I am a hands‑on digital hardware engineer with a background in both academic research and industrial product development. I trained at the National Technical University of Athens (Dipl.-Ing.), completed an M.Sc. in Biomedical Engineering and a D.I.C. at Imperial College London, and pursued a PhD on ultra low‑power bioelectronics and analogue VLSI.

During my PhD with the Bio‑Inspired VLSI Circuits and Systems Group at Imperial College London, my research primarily focused on the development of ultra low-power integrated BioElectronics for the continuous-time, continuous-value computation of highly non-linear cellular and molecular dynamics.

After my PhD I contributed to applied research and engineering projects, including:
- **Wellcome Trust / Department of Health Healthcare Innovation Challenge** (2012–2014) — PCB design and layout, programming and testing of electronic devices for real‑time patient monitoring.  

- **EPSRC project: electronic‑based ELISA with microfluidics** (University of Southampton, 2014–2017) — PCB design and layout, microcontroller and FPGA programming and real-time monitoring.

- **EPSRC project: Tomographic imaging of flow and chromophore concentrations in biological tissue** (University College London, 2017–2020) — hardware and firmware for time‑resolved DOT and DCS instrumentation; high‑speed data acquisition, embedded control, and prototype system integration for volumetric blood‑flow and chromophore imaging.

In 2020 I moved into industry, focusing on robust hardware and firmware for demanding, real‑world applications in biomedical devices, automotive systems, satellite communications and defence systems. 

I design production‑grade embedded systems and FPGA architectures, develop multilayer PCBs, and deliver firmware and verification flows that survive field deployment and regulatory scrutiny.
